Privacy Policy

WetPantry Scanner · Last updated 11 September 2026

The short version. WetPantry Scanner has no accounts and no sign-in. Your pantry and your recipes are stored on your device and are not sent anywhere. Three things do leave the device, and only when you act: a barcode you scan, a recipe photo you choose to have read, and a check that the app is genuine. There is no analytics, no advertising, and no tracking of any kind.

What stays on your device

Everything you build up in the app is held in local storage on your iPhone, and nowhere else:

  • Pantry items: barcode, product name, brand, quantity, expiration date, which location you keep them in, and any notes you write.
  • Recipes: the title, the ingredient list, and the photo you took of the page.
  • The locations you create, along with their names, icons, and colours.

None of this is uploaded, backed up to us, or visible to anyone but you. If you use iCloud device backup, your pantry may be included in that backup under Apple's terms, the same as any other app's local data.

What leaves your device

Barcode lookups. When you scan a barcode, the number is sent to Open Food Facts, a public food database, which returns a product name, brand, package size, and photo. Nothing identifying you is attached to that request. The request does identify the app itself and links to this site, because Open Food Facts asks every application to say who it is. The camera image never leaves your device: the barcode is decoded on the phone and only the resulting number is sent.

Recipe scanning. When you photograph a recipe and ask the app to read it, that photo is sent to a service run by the developer, which passes it to Anthropic to extract the ingredient list. Neither that service nor the developer keeps the photo. None of this happens until you have agreed to it on the Recipes tab. Your own copy of it is saved on your device alongside the ingredients that were read from it, so you can check the original against the result; deleting the recipe deletes both. Once Anthropic has it, their handling of it is governed by their own privacy policy, not this one. The extracted ingredients come back to your device and are saved there. This only happens when you take or choose a photo and ask for it to be read.

Proving the app is genuine. Recipe scanning is limited to five scans a day per installation, and that limit has to be enforced somewhere the device cannot simply bypass. The app uses Apple's App Attest to prove it is a real, unmodified copy. This creates an identifier for your installation, which the service stores along with a count of how many scans that installation has used. The Recipes tab explains all of this and asks you to agree before any of it happens; the record is created when you agree, which is before you scan anything, because that screen then shows how many scans you have left. If you never agree, nothing is sent and no record exists. The identifier is generated by Apple for this purpose, is specific to this app, is not your Apple Account, and is not connected to your name, email, or any other app. It cannot be used to identify you as a person.

What we do not do

  • No account, so no name, email address, or password is ever asked for or held.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting services, and no advertising.
  • No tracking, and nothing shared with data brokers or advertisers.
  • No third-party software development kits of any kind are embedded in the app.
  • Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared beyond the two services named above.

Nutrition and allergy information

The app deliberately does not show ingredients, allergens, or nutrition figures. It takes only a product's name, brand, and package size from Open Food Facts, so that nobody is led to rely on crowd-sourced data for a decision it cannot safely support. Always read the package.

Camera and photo access

The app asks for the camera so it can read barcodes and photograph recipes. Barcode frames are processed on the device and discarded. A recipe photo is sent for reading only when you ask for it. If you choose an existing photo instead, the app receives only the image you picked, not your photo library.

Deleting your data

Because there is no account, there is nothing to close. Deleting the app from your device deletes your pantry, your recipes, and the photos stored with them.

The installation identifier is the one thing that does not go with it. That record stays on the service until it is removed by hand, and a reinstalled app generally starts over with a new one. The record is a random identifier and a counter; it carries no name, no address, and nothing about what you scanned or cooked. If you would like it removed, ask through the link below and quote the installation id, which Settings shows under Your Record once recipe scanning is in use. That id is the only way to tell which record is yours; without it the request cannot be acted on. Settings also has a Stop Using Recipe Scanning control, which makes the app forget its key and send nothing further, though the record already created stays until it is removed on request.

Children

WetPantry Scanner is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes
